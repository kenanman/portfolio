PROJECT 01 // FLAGSHIP HARDWARE • 25kV AC (6–15V 65W) DRIVER + 30kV CW MULTIPLIER

OzoCore: 250kV Cold-Plasma Reactor

25kV AC (6–15V / 65W Max) 30kV-Rated: 15 Caps + 20 Diodes Rack & Pinion Controllable Electrodes Full OzoCore Dossier

Synthesizing O3 via a 25kV AC (65W) Driver & 30kV-Rated CW Cascade

I engineered OzoCore as a self-contained high-voltage plasma reactor inside a sealed 25 × 25 × 25 cm (15.625 L) clear acrylic chamber. Output power is governed by a front manual PWM voltage knob (6.0 V to 15.0 V) feeding a 25 kV AC high-frequency driver module (65 W max output). That 25 kV AC output drives a custom Cockcroft-Walton voltage multiplier built from 30 kV-rated parts — 15 ceramic capacitors and 20 high-voltage diodes — vacuum-encapsulated in clear epoxy resin to reach ~250 kV peak potential across controllable dual electrodes mounted on a precision rack-and-pinion drive (enabling continuous mechanical gap spacing adjustment to tune Paschen breakdown voltage and prevent thermal arc-over).

Stage 1 // HV Driver & CW Multiplier
6–15V PWM → 25kV AC (65W) → ~250kV DC

Built with 30 kV-rated components (15 ceramic capacitors + 20 HV diodes) potted in solid epoxy resin (~20 kV/mm dielectric strength) to prevent stage-to-stage air flashover.

Synthesizes ozone inside the 15.625 L chamber while an SC01-O3 sensor triggers a warning at 6 ppm and automatically cuts the HV relay at 8 ppm.

Key Hardware & Firmware Debugging War Stories

WAR STORY 01 // PARASITIC AIR BREAKDOWN ACROSS 15 CAPS & 20 DIODES

When testing the bare 30kV-rated 15-capacitor / 20-diode ladder on the silicone mat, humid air (~3 kV/mm breakdown) permitted parasitic arcing between stages. Solution: Vacuum-potting the entire cascade in two-part clear dielectric epoxy resin (~20 kV/mm dielectric strength) permanently eliminated external corona leakage and EMI resets.

WAR STORY 02 // 38-PIN ESP32 PIN EXHAUSTION & 3× 0x3C OLED COLLISIONS

Driving 9 sensors and 4 displays pushed the 38-pin ESP32 to its physical GPIO limit, and all three 0.96" SSD1306 OLEDs shared address 0x3C. Solution: Mapped GPIO 34–39 strictly as input-only ADC/echo lines, split I²C buses, and wrote a zero-delay() round-robin state machine.

WAR STORY 03 // WHITE-SCREEN IR BUG & PHANTOM 10 PPM SATURATION

Fixed an inverted IR chamber-lift boolean that locked the 2.8" TFT in emergency mode, and resolved a misrouted SC01-O3 analog line that caused false 10 ppm saturation in clean air.

VERIFIED ARCHITECTURE & KINEMATICS

1:7 Scale ABB IRB 460 Parallelogram Mechanics

Design: daGHIZmo / MK2

Unlike conventional serial-link hobbyist arms where every joint motor adds cantilevered dead-weight to the distal links, the EEZYbotARM MK2 concentrates all three heavy MG995 metal-gear servos inside the stationary base turret. Motion is transmitted through a dual closed-loop 4-bar parallelogram linkage that mechanically locks the end-effector wrist strictly parallel to the ground (θwrist = 0°) across the entire vertical and radial reach envelope.

PRECISION REAMING & LOCKNUT PRELOAD

FDM-printed pinholes exhibit ±0.15 mm thermal shrinkage that either binds the 4-bar linkage or introduces slop at the gripper tip. I precision-reamed every pivot bore and preloaded each joint with M3/M4 Nyloc locknuts and brass bushings.

CUBIC PWM TRAJECTORY INTERPOLATION

Stepping an MG995 servo directly from θ0 to θf spikes current above 2.5A and shakes the chassis. My C++ controller interpolates joint angles in 5 ms time-slices with trapezoidal acceleration ramps.
