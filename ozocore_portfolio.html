OzoCore 250 kV

High-Voltage Cold-Plasma Ozone Synthesis & 9-Sensor Autonomous Safety Reactor

OzoCore is a self-contained high-voltage plasma reactor I designed, 3D-printed, hand-soldered, and programmed from scratch to study corona-discharge ozone synthesis (3O2 → 2O3) inside a sealed 25 × 25 × 25 cm (15.625 L) clear acrylic chamber. Rather than treating ozone generation as an unmonitored open spark gap, OzoCore pairs an epoxy-potted 250 kV Cockcroft-Walton multiplier with controllable dual electrodes mounted on a precision rack-and-pinion drive (enabling continuous mechanical gap spacing adjustment to tune Paschen breakdown voltage and prevent thermal arc-over) and a deterministic ESP32 safety supervisor monitoring 9 sensors across 4 live displays.

SECTION 02 // HIGH-VOLTAGE POWER ELECTRONICS & PLASMA CHEMISTRY

From 6–15V PWM Input to 250 kV Corona Discharge

How OzoCore steps a low-voltage DC input up to ~250,000 Volts to split diatomic oxygen (O2) into triatomic ozone (O3) without arc flashovers.

STAGE 01 // INPUT CONTROL

6–15V Manual PWM Knob

A front-mounted 10A PWM rotary controller modulates the DC supply rail between 6.0 V and 15.0 V, monitored live on a dedicated 3-digit 7-segment LED voltmeter.

STAGE 02 // HV AC DRIVER

25 kV AC Module (65W Max)

The 6–15V rail feeds a high-frequency 25 kV AC step-up module rated up to 65 Watts maximum output at 15V, gated by an ESP32 safety relay.

STAGE 03 // CW MULTIPLIER

15 Caps + 20 Diodes (30kV)

A custom Cockcroft-Walton voltage multiplier built from 30 kV-rated components (15 ceramic capacitors and 20 HV diodes) Vacuum-potted in clear epoxy resin to reach ~250 kV.

STAGE 04 // RACK & PINION ELECTRODES

Controllable Corona Gap

Inside the 15.6 L chamber, dual electrodes are mounted on a precision rack-and-pinion drive. Mechanically controlling the gap spacing fine-tunes field intensity (\(E = V/d\)) to maximize violet corona ionization (O2 → 2O• → O3) while preventing thermal arc-overs.

STAGE 05 // CLOSED-LOOP

8 ppm Auto Cut-Off

The SC01-O3 electrochemical sensor tracks chamber concentration from 0–10 ppm, triggering a warning at 6 ppm and cutting the HV relay at 8 ppm.

Why 30 kV-Rated Parts & Epoxy Encapsulation Were Essential

15 CAPS • 20 DIODES

Stepping a 25 kV AC high-frequency driver (6–15V input, 65W max) up to ~250 kV requires a multi-stage Cockcroft-Walton charge-pump cascade. During each half-cycle of the 25 kV AC input, charge is transferred progressively up the ladder of 15 high-voltage ceramic capacitors through 20 directional high-voltage rectifier diodes, each rated for 30 kV to provide safety margin above the 25 kV stage-to-stage swing.

Dielectric Breakdown
Air: ~3 kV/mm vs Epoxy: ~20 kV/mm

Potted in clear epoxy resin to eliminate external corona flashovers and lock 30kV parts securely.

Rack & Pinion Control
Paschen Gap Actuation (d)

Mechanical rack-and-pinion adjusts electrode gap distance \(d\) to optimize \(E = V/d\) and prevent arc breakdown.

Plasma Stoichiometry
3 O₂ + Energy → 2 O₃

Non-thermal corona splits O₂ into radical oxygen without thermal degradation.

SECTION 03 // EMBEDDED TELEMETRY & DEFENSE-IN-DEPTH SAFETY

9 Sensors • 38-Pin ESP32 Supervisor • 4 Displays • 4 Shut-Off Paths

Because high-voltage plasma and concentrated ozone are both hazardous, OzoCore is architected so that no single software bug or operator oversight can leave the 250 kV multiplier energized in an unsafe state.

INPUTS // 9 SENSORS + 3 CONTROLS 3.3V LOGIC
  • 01.
    SC01-O3 (Electrochemical): Primary chamber ozone sensor (0–10 ppm range).
  • 02.
    MQ-135 (Air Quality): Oxidizing gas & air contaminant sensor (raw ADC 0–4095).
  • 03.
    MQ-2 (Smoke / Combustible): Detects smoke or arcing insulation byproducts inside the chamber.
  • 04.
    DHT22 (Temp & Humidity): Tracks thermal buildup (°C) and relative humidity (%RH).
  • 05.
    IR Chamber-Lift Interlock: Optical barrier under the acrylic lid — trips HV instantly if lifted.
  • 06–09.
    4× HC-SR04 Ultrasonics: 360° perimeter proximity array (< 30 cm spectator alert).
CORE // 38-PIN ESP32 SUPERVISOR NON-BLOCKING

A single 38-pin ESP32 orchestrates all 9 sensors, 4 displays, the 24-LED addressable status ring, the buzzer, and the HV safety relay using a deterministic millis() round-robin state machine (zero delay() calls).

4 INDEPENDENT SHUT-OFF PATHS (DEFENSE IN DEPTH)
1. Rear Master Hardware Switch

Physically breaks the main power rail at the rear of the chassis — completely independent of the ESP32.

Firmware-controlled relay opens within milliseconds if O₃ ≥ 8 ppm, Temp ≥ 35°C, MQ ≥ 3000, or the acrylic chamber is lifted.

SECTION 06 // FIRMWARE DESIGN & REAL HARDWARE DEBUGGING WAR STORIES

6 Real Hardware & Firmware Bugs I Solved Building OzoCore

Integrating a 250 kV plasma source with 9 sensors and 4 displays on a single 38-pin ESP32 exposed real-world hardware constraints that never show up in textbook schematics.

RULE 01 // ZERO BLOCKING
No delay() in Main Loop

All sensor polling, OLED refreshes, and LED ring animations run on independent millis() timers so safety cut-offs respond instantly.

RULE 02 // ROUND-ROBIN SONAR
Sequential HC-SR04 Pings

Pinging all 4 ultrasonic sensors at once caused acoustic cross-talk; a round-robin state machine fires one sensor per time slice.

RULE 03 // DIRTY-RECT TFT
Flicker-Free SPI Updates

The 2.8" ILI9341 TFT only redraws numerical regions when values change, reserving full-screen fills strictly for emergency state transitions.

RULE 04 // HARD INTERLOCK
Relay Preempts Everything

Safety evaluation runs at the top of every cycle and forces the HV relay pin LOW before any display or serial task executes.

BUG 01 // GPIO EXHAUSTION

Running Out of Pins on a 38-Pin ESP32

Between the SPI TFT, 3 OLEDs, 4 ultrasonic sensors, 3 analog gas sensors, DHT22, IR sensor, WS2812B ring, buzzer, and HV relay, I hit the ESP32’s physical pin ceiling. I had to carefully map GPIO 34–39 strictly as input-only ADC/echo lines and avoid strapping pins (GPIO 0, 2, 12, 15) that prevented the MCU from booting.

BUG 02 // I²C ADDRESS COLLISION

Three OLEDs Hardwired to 0x3C

All three 0.96" SSD1306 OLEDs came hardwired to the exact same I²C address (0x3C). Putting them on one bus caused them to mirror each other or corrupt frames. I partitioned the ESP32’s two hardware I²C controllers (Wire and Wire1) / multiplexer channels, and tracked down a flaky jumper wire that was throwing intermittent NACK freezes.

BUG 03 // THE WHITE-SCREEN LOCKUP

Inverted IR Chamber-Lift Polarity

During early integration, the 2.8" TFT kept flashing solid white/red and locking the reactor in emergency cut-off even with the acrylic chamber seated. Debugging revealed the IR obstacle module pulls its output LOW when blocked (chamber seated) and HIGH when lifted — the exact opposite of my initial boolean check.

BUG 04 // PHANTOM 10 PPM SATURATION

SC01-O3 Reading 10 ppm in Fresh Air

On first power-up, the SC01-O3 sensor immediately reported 10.0 ppm (SATURATED) in clean room air and tripped the HV relay. Tracing the harness with a multimeter revealed the analog signal wire was landed on an adjacent pin held high; moving it to the dedicated ADC1 input restored clean 0.0–0.1 ppm baseline readings.

BUG 05 // 5V VS. 3.3V LOGIC DISCIPLINE

Protecting ESP32 Inputs After a 5V Casualty

An earlier dust sensor was permanently damaged during prototyping when 5V power and 3.3V signal lines were mixed up. From that point forward, I enforced a strict dual-rail wiring standard: sensors requiring 5V heaters (MQ-2, MQ-135) have their outputs scaled/clamped so no ESP32 GPIO ever sees above 3.3 V.

BUG 06 // 250kV CORONA EMI & FLASHOVER

Solving Air Breakdown with Epoxy Potting

Before encapsulation, the 15-capacitor / 20-diode 30kV CW ladder hissed and arced between stages in humid air, radiating broadband EMI that glitched the I²C displays. Vacuum-potting the entire multiplier in clear epoxy resin (~20 kV/mm dielectric strength) and isolating it outside the electronics bay eliminated both stage flashover and MCU resets.
